\title{Temporary Ranking Reversals in State-Space Session Recommendation}
\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\institute{Anonymous Institution}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We test whether a selective state-space encoder becomes more competitive as
session-recommendation data grows. On public Diginetica, GRU4Rec and a local
pure-PyTorch selective-SSM proxy share the same head, loss, validation
procedure, and training budget. We evaluate four training sizes, three
independent data draws, and three model seeds. The SSM proxy is slightly ahead
on all draws at 15K sessions, but every paired interval contains zero. At 30K,
it wins one draw and loses two, with mean Recall@20 0.0987 versus 0.1093 for
GRU. Three of nine 30K SSM runs also converge to substantially weaker
solutions. The evidence supports a temporary, draw-sensitive ranking reversal,
not a stable scaling crossover. The proxy is not a reproduction of Mamba4Rec
or SIGMA, and no state-of-the-art claim is made.
\keywords{Session recommendation \and State-space models \and Reproducibility}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Architecture comparisons usually vary initialization seeds while holding one
training split fixed. In sparse recommendation, the sampled sessions also
change item coverage and transition evidence. We ask whether the relative
ranking of GRU4Rec and a local selective-SSM proxy persists across both data
draws and model seeds.

\section{Related Work}
GRU4Rec introduced recurrent session recommendation; SASRec, BERT4Rec, and
SR-GNN established attention and graph alternatives. Mamba4Rec and SIGMA apply
selective state-space components to recommendation. Our proxy is deliberately
scoped and must not be interpreted as a reproduction of either complete
architecture.

\section{Protocol}
Both encoders use the same Diginetica preprocessing, item embeddings,
full-softmax head, objective, validation queries, early stopping, and budget.
Training subsets contain 10K, 12K, 15K, or 30K sessions. Each size uses data
draws 42, 123, and 456 and model seeds 42, 123, and 456, yielding 72 primary
runs. Query-level paired bootstrap intervals are computed after averaging
model seeds within each fixed draw. They quantify uncertainty conditional on
that draw; cross-draw robustness is assessed from the three draw effects.

At 15K, draw-specific SSM-minus-GRU differences are +.0020, +.0002, and
+.0016; all paired intervals contain zero. At 30K, differences are -.0416
(95\% CI [-.0502,-.0331]), -.0109 ([-.0189,-.0027]), and +.0204
([+.0111,+.0298]). All draws temporarily reverse sign between 12K and 15K,
but only draw 456 remains positive at 30K. This non-monotonic pattern does not
support a stable scaling crossover.

Six of nine 30K SSM runs reach Recall@20 at least .10; three converge between
.008 and .016. A bounded contractive recurrence changes seed-level outcomes
but does not improve aggregate accuracy or robustness.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
A single 15K draw could produce a misleading architecture conclusion. The
result applies only to the evaluated local proxy, not official Mamba4Rec or
SIGMA. The primary study uses one public dataset and a fixed test set; a
full-scale official-model comparison on a second public dataset remains
necessary for external validity. A capped RetailRocket pipeline check is kept
as an artifact but excluded from scientific comparisons.

\section{Reproducibility}
The primary factorial matrix contains 72 runs. The audit covers 136 records
because it also includes architecture and contractive-SSM diagnostics, which
are not treated as additional primary replications. Manifests, predictions,
paired inference, crossover analysis, and variance decomposition are included.
